You search a representative's name. The profile shows how many votes
they cast this year, how many they missed, how often they broke with
their party. A heatmap shows when they're actually showing up. Scroll
down for the bills they're sponsoring or moving, click into one, and
you see the action timeline next to a strip of who lobbied for or
against it and how much money flowed.

The audience is the politically-engaged citizen and the civic-curious
journalist who wanted to know what's happening but didn't want to learn
the federal websites.

## What's behind it

452,000 events stitched together from primary government sources:
15,500 bills, 134,000 lobbying filings, 31,000 floor speeches, 42,000
roll-call votes, 1,100 campaign-finance rows tied to bill activity.
Daily incremental updates from govinfo BILLSTATUS bulk, clerk.house.gov,
senate.gov, congress.gov, the LDA database, the FEC, and the
unitedstates/congress-legislators YAML registry.

Read-time validation on lobbying mentions filters around 44% of stale
boilerplate. Lobbyists routinely keep copying old bill numbers across
Congresses, and naive matching credits them with influence they no
longer have.

## Stack

FastAPI worker plus DuckDB on a Railway Hobby plan. Daily cron runs an
asyncio subprocess scheduler at 07:00 UTC. OG share previews, sitemap,
autocomplete search. Open-source under MIT.
