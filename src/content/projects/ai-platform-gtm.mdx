I run paid acquisition for a consumer AI startup. I'm keeping the client
unnamed. I took over an empty ad account with one number to hit: keep the cost
of a good signup under target while daily volume climbs about a hundredfold.
Everything downstream reports to that number.

## Finding the personas

Nobody handed me the audience. The first read was an older, deliberate
customer, the opposite of an impulse buyer. Later I went through the
conversations people were actually having with the product, and two sharper
personas fell out of them. Each got its own campaign and its own creative. I let
the ad find its person instead of stacking interest targets, which Meta reads as
low quality anyway.

## Moving with the pivots

Most of the job is keeping up with the product. The conversion event used to be
an email signup. Now it's a live chat with the product, so every campaign got
rebuilt around click-to-message and every hook got rewritten to invite a text.
The map grew past the launch cities into the major US metros. None of that was
allowed to cost efficiency, and the same budget kept getting pushed harder.

## Meta and Google

Meta carries the account. One identity creative hit the target cost per signup,
and I've held it there through every re-learning window.

Google Search took more work. I ran it, then rebuilt it. Its expensive
high-intent searches were the wrong intent. I cut them. The budget went to
cheaper discovery searches, and it still couldn't reach a competitive cost per
signup for a free product. So I paused it and moved the spend to where it
converts.

## Treating it as data

Most growth advice is folklore. I don't trust the dashboard. I capture the
primary numbers myself and check attribution at the source, which means a shift
in the market or on a platform shows up for me early. AI goes where it earns its
keep: creative exploration, competitor monitoring. I built a pipeline that
generates image and video creative, and a Chrome extension for competitor
research. Organic content is the next thing I'm testing.
