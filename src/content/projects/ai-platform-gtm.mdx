I took over paid acquisition for a consumer AI startup with an empty ad account
and one target: keep the cost of a good signup under a fixed number while volume
scaled. I'm keeping the client unnamed, so every figure here is relative.

In the first four months, cost per lead fell about 60% while monthly spend grew
4.7×, and monthly leads went up eightfold. Spend and efficiency usually pull
against each other, so that's the result I'm proudest of. Later the client
tightened the conversion event to completed signups only, which pushed the cost
per lead up on paper. I report the account against both definitions instead of
quoting the flattering one. Since then the monthly budget ceiling has gone up to
about 3.5× what we spent in July.

## One line of copy, 2.5× cheaper

My biggest creative lesson came from a clean accident. Two ads ran side by side
in one ad set that week with the same visual, and only one line of copy
differed. One named a pain the customer already felt. It got 2.3× the
click-through rate and cost 2.5× less per lead than its twin, which described
what the product promised. "Name the pain, not the promise" became the rule
every concept since has been built on, and it's a big part of how I ship around
five new ads a week without guessing.

## The Instagram gap

Instagram and Facebook had the same CPM and the same click-through rate, but an
Instagram lead cost more than twice as much. Everything before the click looked
fine. It showed up on the landing page, where Instagram visitors converted at
well under half the rate. So I picked up a phone and tapped the ad. Instagram's
in-app browser opens logged out of Google, which means "Sign in with Google"
sends people back to typing everything in by hand. That one was a product fix,
not a media one.

## My own source of truth

Most growth advice is folklore, and Ads Manager doesn't help much: it buries the
per-variant breakdowns you need to know which headline actually did the work. I
built my own warehouse that pulls the Meta Marketing API into DuckDB every day
at the level of individual headlines and body copy. Dashboards and health checks
sit on top. When a site change killed the tracking pixel for two and a half
days, the warehouse flagged it as a tracking failure instead of letting it read
as a collapse, and raw signups over the same days proved nothing real had been
lost.

My toolkit also launches campaigns, carefully. Anything it creates starts
paused, under hard spend caps per ad set and per launch, and only inside an
allowlist of campaigns, with fourteen tests guarding those rules. Video ads
render from code in a Remotion pipeline, and still images come out of a
generation pipeline with its own quality checks.

## Reading results honestly

It saved real money more than once. Our best creative once
cost twice as much when the budget squeezed it into four mid-size metros as it
did running across seventeen. That was a geography problem, not a creative one.
Saying so freed budget instead of burning a winning concept. Google Search got
the same treatment: for a free product it couldn't reach a competitive cost per
signup, so I paused it and put the money back into Meta.
