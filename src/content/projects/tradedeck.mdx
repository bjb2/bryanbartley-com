You open the lab. A strategy backtest loads in the center: equity curve
against the S&P, durability metrics in a strip, exit reasons broken out
by count. The right rail is every backtest you've ever saved, sorted by
whichever metric you trust today. Click one to load it; ctrl-click a
second to overlay them normalised at start. When something holds up
across decades, the daily tab generates today's manual order list
formatted for the broker you actually trade through.

The audience is the self-directed investor who wants to test investment
theses against real market history before betting on them, and who has
stopped trusting any single number.

## How it stays honest

Tradedeck sits over a Python research pipeline that owns the data and
the backtester. SQLite is the seam between them, so the pipeline keeps
running unattended and the UI never gets in its way. The deck is the
place you actually use: define a thesis, run the backtest, compare it
against the ones you've already run, decide.

Live positions ship with the exact trail stop the strategy was
backtested with. The wizard locks the broker override out, so what runs
in real money can't silently drift away from what survived the
simulation.

## Aesthetic

Bloomberg terminal on purpose. Pitch ink canvas, phosphor green for
gains, crimson for losses, terminal amber as the principal voice,
JetBrains Mono everywhere data lives, zero rounded corners. P&L colors
are reserved for P&L; equity-chart lines are amber, cyan, and slate, so
"is this winning" is never confused with "which line is which".
