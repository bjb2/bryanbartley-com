I run paid acquisition for Outgoing, a startup building an AI agent that finds
and books real-world things to do. I took over an empty ad account with one
number to hit: keep the cost of a good signup under target while daily volume
climbs from around ten toward a hundred. Everything downstream reports to that
number.

## Finding the personas

The audience wasn't handed to me. The starting read was a 35-to-55 crowd that
wants to get out more, the opposite of the what's-happening-tonight impulse
buyer. Later I went through the conversations people were starting with the
product itself, and two sharper personas fell out: someone planning a date
night, and someone planning a family weekend. Each became its own campaign with
its own creative, and I let the ad self-select the right person rather than
stacking interest targets that Meta reads as low quality.

## Moving with the pivots

The target keeps moving, and keeping up with it is most of the job. The
conversion event changed from an email signup to a live chat with the product,
so every campaign got rebuilt around click-to-message and every hook rewritten
to invite a text. The map went from a few launch cities to the major US metros.
Positioning the company decided against, like price and accessibility angles,
had to come back out of creative that was already running. Every one of those
changes resets Meta's learning phase, so the craft is holding efficiency steady
while the ground moves under it.

## Meta and Google

Meta carries the account. The winning identity creative hit the target cost per
signup, and I hold it there through each re-learning window. Google Search I ran,
diagnosed, and rebuilt: the expensive "events this weekend" searches were the
wrong intent and got cut, while the cheaper discovery-framed searches like "cool
experiences near me" got the budget. When Google still couldn't reach a
competitive cost per signup for a free product, I paused it and moved the spend
to where it converts.

## Treating it as data, not folklore

Most growth advice is folklore. What beats it is refusing to trust the
dashboard. I capture the primary numbers myself and verify attribution at the
source, which turns a tracking failure into a same-day catch instead of a
three-week mystery. That paid off directly the week a site change silently
killed the tracking pixel for two and a half days: platform numbers cratered
while raw signups held flat, so I could call it an attribution illusion and talk
the client out of a panicked budget cut. The custom data layer over Meta's API
and the programmatic video pipeline both exist to serve that habit, surfacing
the per-variant signal the ad managers bury.
