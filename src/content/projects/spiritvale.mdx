SpiritVale is an indie MMORPG on Steam. I started on the outside, building
[SpiritValers](/work/spiritvalers), its community wiki. That got me into the
game's repository. Now I work on the game under contract. I design the UI, then
build it myself in Unity and C#, along with the client and server code behind it.

My first commit landed on November 3. Patch 0.35.3 went out the next day with
five of my changes in its notes. By 0.36.2 on November 16, 96 of my pull
requests had shipped across five patches.

## What shipped

- **Trading.** A new Market with Browse, Sell and History tabs: list up to 30
  items at once, search by stat rolls, check a price guide that compares
  listings like yours, and look back over up to 90 days of sales. Storage was
  rebuilt too, with saved filters that work in both screens.
- **Finding your way.** A new minimap whose icons say what each NPC does, and a
  world map that works as a guide. Search any map, monster, item or NPC, and
  click a monster for its stats and drops.
- **Playing together.** A chat window with emotes, item links that work across
  servers, and per-tab region filters. Party Search across regions, a region
  picker, leaderboards with weekly boards and Spire clears, and guild rename.
- **Your character.** Floating Equipment, Status, Inventory and Skills windows.
  Loadouts, a cosmetics store and wardrobe, a redesigned character select,
  character rename, and a death recap that shows what killed you.
- **Every screen.** NPC shops and crafting on one design system, with baskets,
  carts and Craft ×N. Dialogue moved onto paper cards. A Settings window with
  search, light and dark themes plus a theme picker, UI scale for small
  screens, and four more languages brought up to the level of Chinese.
- **Performance.** A cap on how many players get drawn in a crowd. Each map
  also loads about 518 MB less texture memory.

Where a feature needed the server, I built that part too. Guild rename takes
the Stars and renames the guild in one transaction. The Market's sales history
lives on the data server.

## The loot filter

Players decide which drops get picked up by their loot key and their pet. They
also decide how a drop looks and sounds on the ground. A drop-rate threshold
covers most of it. Any category can override that threshold to collect
everything or to leave drops for manual pickup. Equipment and artifacts can also
keep only drops with a minimum number of max-value stats.

Personal rules sit on top of all that. The first match wins. A rule can target a
whole category or narrow down to one of roughly 850 items. It can also test the
rolled stats, like crit damage at least +7% or MP cost at most -10%. Values come
from a dropdown of what the stat can actually roll, the way the auction house
filter works, so nobody types a threshold that can never hit. A matching rule
can force the pickup and set the drop's line color and sound.

The page previews ground loot with the same evaluator the server runs. On the
server, every drop is checked against each player's own filter. That includes
parties on Even Share, where a member whose filter rejects a drop gets skipped.
Rules never hide anything: click a drop and it's yours.

## The login screen

The old login screen was a region table next to a wall of patch-note text. Now a
side rail shows each region's ping and how many players are online. Behind it,
the key art crossfades every seven seconds. The latest three Steam announcements
sit as cards, each wearing the art from its own Steam post, and open into a full
panel. Every new string ships in all 17 of the game's languages.
